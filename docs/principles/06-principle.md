# Open by default

## Statement

Specifications, standards and, where possible, software and governance are open and publicly available.

## Rationale

Openness lowers cost, prevents vendor lock-in and builds trust between countries. Shared infrastructure must not become a source of revenue for a single party.

## Implications

- Interface specifications and implementation guides are published openly.
- Open source is preferred for shared regional components.
- Architecture decisions are documented and public (see [architecture decision records](../reference-architecture/architecture-decision-records.md)).
- Shared infrastructure remains under public or collective governance.

<small>Source: New principle. Aligned with EIF openness and transparency ([EC 2017](../references.md#ref-ec2017)), digital public infrastructure ([WHO & ITU 2020](../references.md#ref-whoitu2020)), the Dutch national health data network principles on open source and public control ([VWS 2025](../references.md#ref-vws2025)) and the Principles for Digital Development ([Digital Impact Alliance n.d.](../references.md#ref-pdd)).</small>
