# Secure and trusted

## Statement

People and organisations can trust that health data shared across borders is protected, used only for agreed purposes, and handled under explicit agreements.

## Rationale

Cross-border exchange involves several legal jurisdictions with different data protection rules. Without explicit trust, organisations will not share data and people will not accept it.

## Implications

- Privacy and security are built in by design and by default.
- Only the data needed for the stated purpose is exchanged; there is no bulk exchange.
- Authentication (Who are you?) and authorisation (What can you see?) are completed before any data retrieval request is processed.
- Access decisions consider the purpose of use (e.g. emergency vs. routine), the healthcare provider, the facility and the patient's consent status.
- All access is logged for audit, and people can see who accessed their data.
- Participants operate under a shared trust framework that sets out roles, responsibilities and legal agreements, including controller and processor roles across jurisdictions.
- Each exchange complies with the data protection law of every jurisdiction involved.
